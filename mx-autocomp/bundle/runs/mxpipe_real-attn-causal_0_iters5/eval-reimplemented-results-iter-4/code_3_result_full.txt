### Optimization Plan

We will apply **Strategy 4** (Tiling the entire attention pipeline along the $I$ / query dimension) to maximize CPU cache locality and reduce DRAM traffic.

#### Inefficiencies in the Baseline Code:
1. **DRAM Bandwidth Bottleneck:** The baseline code executes $S_1 = Q \times K^T$ for the entire $128 \times 128$ matrix, drains all of it to DRAM, processes the entire matrix on the CPU for softmax, writes the $128 \times 128$ quantized $P_q$ back to DRAM, and then loads it back to the Gemmini scratchpad for $O = P \times V$. This results in massive DRAM read/write overhead.
2. **Poor CPU Cache Locality:** The CPU-side softmax sweeps over the entire $128 \times 128$ matrix (32 KB for $S_1$ as BF16, plus space for $P_q$), evicting cache lines and causing CPU stalls.

#### Optimization Strategy:
We will tile the entire attention pipeline along the $I$ (query row) dimension with a block size of $DIM = 16$. For each of the $8$ tiles ($128 / 16 = 8$ iterations):
1. **Compute $Q_{\text{tile}} \times K^T$:**
   * $Q_{\text{tile}}$ is of size $16 \times 64$ ($1 \times 4$ tiles).
   * $K^T$ is of size $64 \times 128$ ($4 \times 8$ tiles).
   * Output $S_{1,\text{tile}}$ is of size $16 \times 128$ ($1 \times 8$ tiles).
   * We load the scale factors for $Q$ and $K^T$, mvin $Q_{\text{tile}}$ and $K^T$ into the scratchpad, compute on Gemmini, and drain $S_{1,\text{tile}}$ directly to its corresponding DRAM slice.
2. **CPU-Side Softmax:**
   * Perform the float-free integer softmax only on the 16 rows of the current tile.
   * Because $S_{1,\text{tile}}$ was just written by Gemmini, it is hot in the CPU's L2/L1 cache.
   * Write the output scale factors ($P\_scales$) and quantized results ($P\_q$) for only the current 16 rows.
3. **Compute $P_{\text{tile}} \times V$:**
   * $P_{\text{tile}}$ is of size $16 \times 128$ ($1 \times 8$ tiles).
   * $V$ is of size $128 \times 64$ ($8 \times 4$ tiles).
   * Output $O_{\text{tile}}$ is of size $16 \times 64$ ($1 \times 4$ tiles).
   * Load the scale factors for $P$ and $V$, mvin $P_{\text{tile}}$ and $V$ into the scratchpad, compute on Gemmini, and drain directly to the corresponding DRAM slice of $O\_hw$.

By interleaving Gemmini computations and CPU softmax at a 16-row granularity, the intermediate activations fit entirely within the CPU's local cache hierarchy.
