An elegant and highly effective optimization plan to improve the execution time of the matrix multiplication kernel is to **eliminate redundant computation and data movement (Strategy 1)** by hoisting loop-invariant operations out of the inner loops.

### Inefficiency Analysis
In the current baseline implementation:
1. **Redundant Scale Loading (A-side)**: In every iteration of the inner loop `j0`, the activation scale factors `A_s` are copied from `A_scales_row` and loaded via `gemmini_mx_load_scales`. However, `A_s` only depends on the outer loop index `i0` and remains completely unchanged across different column blocks `j0`.
2. **Redundant DRAM-to-Scrpad Transfers (A-side)**: The tiles of `A_in` are loaded into scratchpad address `a_base` inside the inner `j0` loop. Since `A_in` tiles only depend on `i0`, they are re-read from DRAM `BLOCKS_N` times.
3. **Coexistence in Scratchpad**: Because `a_base` (0) and `b_base` (`8192 - tiles * tiles_K * DIM`) do not overlap, and the scratchpad size (256 KB) is large enough to hold both A and B tiles simultaneously, the hoisted A tiles will persist safely in the scratchpad across inner loop iterations.
4. **Redundant Configurations**: `gemmini_config_st` is called repeatedly inside the loop, even though the store stride is constant.

### Optimization Plan

1. **Hoist Store Configuration**: Move the invariant output configuration `gemmini_config_st(BLK_OUTC * sizeof(out_t))` outside of all loops (right after `gemmini_extended3_config_ex`).
2. **Hoist Activation Scales**: Move the `memcpy` staging of `A_s` and the subsequent scale load `gemmini_mx_load_scales((uint64_t)A_s, A_S_BYTES, 0)` from the inner loop `j0` to the top of the outer loop `i0`.
3. **Hoist Activation Data MVIN**: Move the `gemmini_config_ld(MATMUL_K * sizeof(elem_t))` and the double-nested loop for loading `A_in` into the scratchpad from the inner loop `j0` to the outer loop `i0` (immediately after loading the A scales).
4. **Retain Variant Operations**: Keep `B_s` staging, `B_in` scratchpad loading, and the compute/readout steps inside the inner `j0` loop since they depend directly on the column block being processed.

This plan maintains strict semantic equivalence and correct programming sequence, while eliminating substantial CPU overhead and redundant DRAM bandwidth.
