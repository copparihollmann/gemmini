## Plan

### Strategy: Hoist scale loads out of the inner loop

**For A scales (`sel=0`):** The A scales depend only on `i0` (which rows of A are being processed). They do not change as `j0` varies. So `gemmini_mx_load_scales` for A (and the corresponding `memcpy` into `A_s`) should be hoisted out of the `j0` loop into the `i0` loop.

**For B scales (`sel=1`):** The B scales depend only on `j0` (which columns of B are being processed). They do not change as `i0` varies. However, since the inner loop iterates over `j0`, and the programming model sequence requires both A and B scales to be loaded before each `gemmini_loop_ws_spad`, we cannot hoist B scales outside the `i0` loop without restructuring. But we *can* hoist A scales out of the `j0` loop — this halves the number of A scale load operations.

**Restructuring:**
1. Move `memcpy(&A_s[g][0], ...)` and `gemmini_mx_load_scales(A_s, A_S_BYTES, 0)` to execute once per `i0` iteration, before the `j0` loop begins.
2. Keep `memcpy(&B_s[g][0], ...)` and `gemmini_mx_load_scales(B_s, B_S_BYTES, 1)` inside the `j0` loop.
3. The programming model sequence is still respected: both scale loads occur before `gemmini_loop_ws_spad`. The A scale load happens earlier (at `i0` start) but remains valid for all `j0` iterations sharing the same `i0`, because `gemmini_loop_ws_spad` consumes whichever scales are currently in `mx_scale_a_mem` — and those are correct for this `i0`.
4. Remove the `gemmini_flush(0)` from the inner loop (it's redundant per-block; one flush at startup suffices) — but since this is a separate strategy concern, we limit to the scale hoisting only.

This reduces A-scale `memcpy` + `gemmini_mx_load_scales` calls from `BLOCKS_M * BLOCKS_N` to `BLOCKS_M`, and B-scale calls from `BLOCKS_M * BLOCKS_N` to `BLOCKS_N` (net: `BLOCKS_M + BLOCKS_N` instead of `BLOCKS_M * BLOCKS_N`).
