### Optimization Plan

1. **Scratchpad Partitioning (Double-Buffering):**
   Partition the 8192-row scratchpad into two equal logical halves to prevent write conflicts:
   - **Buffer 0 (for $Q \times K^T$):** `a_base = 0`, `b_base_end = 4096`, and B is placed at `b_base = 3840` (top of Buffer 0, growing downward).
   - **Buffer 1 (for $P \times V$):** `a_base = 4096`, `b_base_end = 8192`, and B is placed at `b_base = 7936` (top of Buffer 1, growing downward).

2. **Asynchronous DMA Prefetching (Overlap Compute and Memory):**
   - The CPU-based softmax loop is a massive bottleneck that runs entirely on the host. During this time, the Gemmini coprocessor is idle.
   - Right *before* the CPU enters the softmax loop for head $h$, issue asynchronous DMA loads (`gemmini_extended_mvin`) to load:
     - Matrix $V$ of head $h$ into Buffer 1.
     - Matrices $Q$ and $K^T$ of head $h+1$ into Buffer 0 (if $h+1 < H$).
   - The DMA engine will fetch these tiles from DRAM in parallel while the host CPU is busy calculating the softmax.
   - When the softmax finishes, we only need to load the newly computed matrix $P$ (which is very small) and immediately trigger the $P \times V$ compute.
   - In the next iteration ($h+1$), the $Q$ and $K^T$ inputs are already in the scratchpad, allowing us to completely bypass their loading latency and trigger the matmul immediately.

3. **Maximize DMA Width (Block Loading):**
   Since the matrices are contiguous in DRAM along their column dimensions, we can load 4 tiles at once by setting the `cols` parameter of `gemmini_extended_mvin` to `64` instead of `32` or `16`. This reduces the number of DMA commands by half.

4. **Eliminate Redundant Configurations and Cache Flushes:**
