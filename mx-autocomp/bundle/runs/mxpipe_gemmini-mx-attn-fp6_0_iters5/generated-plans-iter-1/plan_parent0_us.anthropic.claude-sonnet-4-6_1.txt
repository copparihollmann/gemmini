The code has two matmul phases (Q×KT and P×V) followed by a CPU-side softmax. Let me focus on the most impactful strategy.

### Strategy Selection: Strategy 12 — Precompute reciprocal of softmax sum

Looking at the softmax loop:
```c
for(int j=0;j<ATT_S;j++) row[j]/=sm;
```

This executes `ATT_S = 128` float divisions per row, and there are `ATT_S = 128` rows, meaning **16,384 float divisions total**. Float division is significantly more expensive than float multiplication on RISC-V (typically 10-20× slower). Replacing each division with a precomputed reciprocal multiplication eliminates 128 divisions per row, replacing them with 1 division + 128 multiplications.

### Plan

**Where:** In the softmax loop (between the two matmul phases), specifically this section:
```c

for(int j=0;j<ATT_S;j++){row[j]=expf2(row[j]-mx);sm+=row[j];}
for(int j=0;j<ATT_S;j++) row[j]/=sm;
```

**Change:** After computing `sm`, compute `float inv_sm = 1.0f / sm;` once, then replace `row[j] /= sm` with `row[j] *= inv_sm` in the normalization loop.

**Result:** Instead of 128 divisions per row (128 rows × 128 divisions = 16,384 divisions), we do 128 multiplications per row plus 1 division per row (128 divisions total + 16,384 multiplications). This is a clear win since multiply is much cheaper than divide.

**Numerical equivalence:** `x * (1/sm)` vs `x / sm` — the difference is at most 1 ULP in the reciprocal, which falls within the "small numerical tolerance" allowed by rule 4. The softmax output feeds into the fp6 quantization step anyway, so this tiny floating-point difference is negligible.
