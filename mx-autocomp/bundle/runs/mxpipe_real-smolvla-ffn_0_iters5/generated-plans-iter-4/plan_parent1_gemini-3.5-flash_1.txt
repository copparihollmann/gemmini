### Optimization Plan

We will apply **Strategy 1: reduce data movement** to eliminate the highly inefficient host-side data staging and redundant scale factor loads.

#### Inefficiencies in the Current Code
1. **Host-Side Data Staging (`memcpy`):** The program packs row and column scales into contiguous CPU buffers (`A_scales_contig` and `B_scales_contig`) using nested loops with `memcpy`. Host-side memory copying is extremely expensive on this accelerator architecture and bottlenecks the system.
2. **Redundant Scale Factor Loads:** The scale factors are loaded inside the loops (`gemmini_mx_load_scales`) for every tile block. Since the scale factors for the entire matrices persist in the hardware's on-chip scale memories (`mx_scale_a_mem` and `mx_scale_b_mem`) until overwritten, they can be loaded just once globally.

#### Optimization Steps
1. **Eliminate Staging Buffers:** Remove `A_scales_contig` and `B_scales_contig` along with their initialization loops and `memcpy` calls.
2. **Hoist Scale Factor Loading:** Move `gemmini_mx_load_scales` completely out of the loops to the very beginning of the `solution` function. We will load the entire `A_scales_row` and `B_scales_col` structures directly from DRAM into the Gemmini scale memory banks `sel=0` and `sel=1` respectively.
3. **Clean Up Loop Nest:** Remove any scale-loading calls from the inner loops to prevent redundant DRAM transactions and eliminate the corresponding latency.
