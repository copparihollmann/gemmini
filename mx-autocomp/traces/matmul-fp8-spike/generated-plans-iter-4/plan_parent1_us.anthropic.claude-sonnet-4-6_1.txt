## Analysis of the Original Code

Looking at the original code, I need to identify which strategy offers the most impactful improvement.

The current `gemmini_mxquant_config_mvout` call uses `lut_update_granularity=1`, meaning the output LUT refreshes after every single tile during the requantization post-pass. This is the most conservative (and slowest) setting. The documentation explicitly states:

> `lut_update_granularity` G in `gemmini_mxquant_config_mvout` controls how often the output LUT refreshes during quantization; **larger G = fewer refreshes = faster**.

Since `out_mx_fmt=3` (BF16 output), the output format is BF16, not a re-quantized FP6/FP4. In this BF16 output path, the LUT refresh is unnecessary overhead on every tile. Setting G to a larger value — specifically `tiles_I * tiles_J` (the total number of output tiles) or simply a very large number — will minimize or eliminate these unnecessary LUT refresh operations entirely.

## Selected Strategy: **Strategy 4 — Try new parameter values**

### Plan

**Change `lut_update_granularity` from `1` to `tiles_I * tiles_J`** in the `gemmini_mxquant_config_mvout` call.

- With `G=1`, the requantizer refreshes the output LUT after every single output tile, incurring overhead `tiles_I * tiles_J` times total.
- With `G = tiles_I * tiles_J`, the LUT refreshes at most once for the entire matmul, amortizing the refresh cost across all output tiles.
- Since `out_mx_fmt=3` (BF16 output), the LUT is not driving the requantization path in a format-sensitive way, making the maximum G value safe for correctness.
- No other changes to the computation, memory layout, or instruction ordering are needed.
