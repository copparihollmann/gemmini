An elegant and effective plan is to apply **Strategy 2: Assign B tiles to a fixed high-address scratchpad region to enable weight-stationary reuse across I tiles**.

### Analysis of Inefficiencies
In the baseline code, the scratchpad utilization is reported as 0KB because `a_base` and `b_base` are not properly defined and allocated in the scratchpad. Without explicit partitioning, the hardware cannot safely manage spatial reuse, leading to redundant memory accesses and lower systolic array utilization. 

With $M=32$, $N=256$, and $K=256$, the tile dimensions are:
- $\text{tiles}_I = 32 / 16 = 2$
- $\text{tiles}_J = 256 / 16 = 16$
- $\text{tiles}_K = 256 / 16 = 16$

The total scratchpad capacity is 8192 rows ($\text{BANK\_NUM} \times \text{BANK\_ROWS} = 4 \times 2048$). 
- A tiles require $\text{tiles}_I \times \text{tiles}_K \times \text{DIM} = 2 \times 16 \times 16 = 512$ rows.
- B tiles require $\text{tiles}_J \times \text{tiles}_K \times \text{DIM} = 16 \times 16 \times 16 = 4096$ rows.
- Combined requirement is $512 + 4096 = 4608$ rows, which fits comfortably within the 8192-row scratchpad limit.

By explicitly partitioning the scratchpad, we can store all B tiles at the high-address end of the scratchpad, allowing them to remain resident and be reused across different I-tiles during weight-stationary execution.

---

### Step-by-Step Optimization Plan

1. **Calculate and Define Scratchpad Bases:**
   * Define `a_base` as `0` at the start of `solution()`.
   * Define `b_base` as `BANK_NUM * BANK_ROWS - tiles_K * tiles_J * DIM` to place B tiles at the very top of the scratchpad, growing downward. This prevents any overlap with A tiles.

2. **Load A Tiles into Scratchpad:**
   * Iterate over `tiles_I` and `tiles_K`.
   * Stream A tiles from DRAM into the scratchpad at the address `a_base + (i * tiles_K + k) * DIM`.

3. **Load B Tiles into High Scratchpad Region:**
   * Iterate over `tiles_J` and `tiles_K`.
   * Stream B tiles from DRAM into the scratchpad at `b_base + (j * tiles_K + k) * DIM`. This matches Rule 12's layout requirement to ensure correct indexing in hardware during compute.

4. **Invoke Compute with Partitioned Scratchpad:**
   * Call `gemmini_loop_ws_spad` using `a_base` as the A base address, and the top boundary sentinel `BANK_NUM * BANK_ROWS` (which is 8192) as the B limit.
   * This partition enables the hardware fused loop to automatically reuse loaded weights and minimize DRAM traffic.
